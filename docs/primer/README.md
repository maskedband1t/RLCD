# Twelve Lessons on Robot Decisions

`index.html` is a standalone, dependency-free primer: how a robot decides what to do next, what the
field is arguing about, and why almost nobody can currently prove their method works. Open it directly
in a browser — no build step, no server.

**Published copy:** https://claude.ai/artifact/72xZUQYi9vMFmXogiLiakd (private)

## Why it exists

This is the teaching layer of the project. The repository's notebooks carry the technical record; this
page carries the explanation, and it is the version to hand to someone who has never touched a robot.

Rules it follows, which the rest of the repo should converge on:

- **No acronym used before it is explained.** VLM and VLA are defined by what goes in and what comes out.
- **One idea and at most one number per lesson.** A spread of 22 points beats a table.
- **Everyday analogies carrying the load** — three speeds of thinking, a stretchy tape measure, phoning
  an expert, a recipe box.
- **Drawn figures, not boxes of prose.** A rectangle full of sentences is still a wall of text.
- **Plain language for the uncomfortable parts too.** Hedged phrasing around a weakness is how a reader
  gets misled, and the weak parts are where a careful reader is actually paying attention.
- **Measured numbers are ours; quoted numbers are named as someone else's.**
